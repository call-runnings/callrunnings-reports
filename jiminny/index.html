Campaign Overview
May 2026 was a month defined more by market mapping than market conversion. The volume of dials was substantial, yet the proportion that turned into genuine two-way conversations was modest, reflecting a market that is largely saturated with competing outreach and, in many cases, already committed to incumbent tools. The conversations that did happen were revealing: a clear divide emerged between prospects who have recently invested in call analysis or coaching technology and are locked in, and those who simply do not prioritise call recording or performance analytics as a discipline at all. Both camps present different challenges and require different responses going forward.

Common Objections
Already under contract with a competitor ~3 conversations
"The prospect recently signed a 12-month contract with Gong and Nooks, so the agent agreed to follow up in 11 months."

Contract lock-in is the single most concrete blocker encountered this month, with Gong appearing repeatedly as the chosen platform. These prospects are not lost — they are timed. Building a follow-up sequence timed to contract renewal windows (typically months 10–11) is essential, and the intervening period should be used to nurture with content that highlights gaps Gong does not address, particularly for non-standard sales models.

Call analysis is not a priority or practice we follow ~3 conversations
"They are not interested in analysing calls or call recordings as they do not actively monitor them. When asked if they have an established process in place, he confirmed that it is simply not something they do."

A meaningful segment of the market has not yet adopted call analysis as a management discipline. This is a category education challenge as much as a product sell. These prospects may require a different entry point — perhaps starting with pain around coaching consistency or forecast accuracy — rather than leading with the technology itself.

Team too small to justify the tool ~2 conversations

Small team size is being used both as a genuine constraint and as a soft rejection. Suzette's follow-up in six months is another version of this — a growth-gated opportunity.

Not the right time — returning in a few months ~3 conversations
"It's not relevant now but could be in three to six months when they expect to have a bigger team."

Timing objections clustered around team growth, budget cycles, and personal transitions. These are warmer than flat rejections and should be managed in a structured follow-up sequence with clear re-engagement dates. Richard (3–6 months), Jason (3–4 months), and Suzette (6 months) all warrant diarised outreach with context carried forward.

We've been contacted before and it wasn't a fit ~2 conversations

Repeat outreach to prospects who have already declined is damaging both to brand perception and to dial efficiency. This points to a CRM or list hygiene issue where prior contact outcomes are not being surfaced before a call is made. Addressing this will reduce wasted dials and prevent unnecessary friction.

Decision is made by a different team or geography ~1 conversations

Organisational structure is diverting conversations away from local contacts who may be users but not buyers. Where prospects flag a centralised or overseas decision-making function, effort should shift quickly to identifying the correct stakeholder rather than attempting to convert an individual with no purchasing authority.

What Prospects Asked
What does the tool do around automation and AI? ~1 conversations

Joshua specifically raised data automation and AI capability as the gaps in his current tool. This is a high-intent signal — he is already in the market and evaluating on the basis of specific feature gaps. Automation and AI-led insights should be foregrounded in the pitch for technically literate prospects who have already adopted a first-generation tool and are looking to upgrade.

Do you have a free or entry-level option? ~1 conversations

Jason's openness to hearing about free options suggests price sensitivity and risk aversion among smaller teams. If a freemium or trial pathway exists, it should be referenced earlier in conversations with small teams as a way to lower the barrier to engagement and allow self-qualification.

Who is this call from and why are you calling? ~1 conversations

The presence of this basic challenge — prospects asking for name and reason before engaging — indicates that the opening seconds of calls are not always landing with sufficient clarity or confidence. A sharper, more immediately credible opening line that signals relevance within the first sentence would reduce the number of calls that stall at this stage.

Can you send me an email overview instead? ~2 conversations

Both Louisa and Jason (in different forms) requested written follow-up rather than continued verbal engagement. This is a deferral rather than a rejection, and should be treated as a warm handoff — the email sent in these cases should be tailored, brief, and include a clear call to action to re-engage rather than being a generic brochure.

Market Themes
Gong dominance is creating a cycle of 12-month lock-ins

Gong appeared in four separate conversations and is consistently being cited as a recently signed or actively used solution, often bundled with other tools as part of a broader tech stack commitment. The 12-month contract structure means that prospects who have adopted Gong are unavailable not merely by preference but by obligation. The opportunity lies in tracking these renewal windows precisely and re-engaging with a differentiated case — particularly around partnership models and AI automation, where Gong was noted as falling short.

A significant segment has not yet adopted call analysis as a practice

Multiple prospects — Martin, Mark, and others — were explicit that call recording and analysis is simply not part of how they manage their teams, and several indicated they do not see a need for it in the future. This represents a category adoption challenge rather than a competitive one. Messaging that leads with the business outcomes of call analysis (coaching consistency, ramp speed, forecast accuracy) rather than the technology itself may be better suited to moving these prospects.

Contact data quality is suppressing effective outreach

Across the month, several calls were lost to prospects who had changed roles, were on extended leave, or had never been the right contact. Calls were made to personal numbers and to individuals who had already been contacted and declined. These are not market signals — they are data quality issues — but they are quietly eroding the efficiency of every dial made and creating friction that reflects on the brand.

Growth-gated interest is creating a genuine pipeline — but one that requires active management

A cluster of prospects — Suzette, Richard, Jason, Joshua — expressed openness contingent on team growth or timing, with specific timeframes mentioned. This is a real and actionable pipeline, but only if those conversations are tracked with context and re-engaged at the right moment. Without structured nurture, these warm signals will cool and the effort invested in the initial conversations will be lost.

Existing customers appearing in cold outreach lists is a risk

Jane was identified mid-call as an existing Jiminny customer, and the interaction revealed a possible data discrepancy in the HubSpot instance. Dialling existing customers as cold prospects risks undermining the relationship and signals an internal data synchronisation gap between the customer base and the outreach list. This warrants an immediate audit.

Recommended Actions
1

Audit the outreach list immediately against the existing customer database to prevent existing clients being dialled as cold prospects — Jane's call this month flagged a live HubSpot discrepancy that needs resolution with Becky and Ollie as a priority.

2

Build a Gong renewal tracker: for every prospect who cited a recent Gong contract, log the contract start date and set a re-engagement reminder for month 10 — Elliot (follow up in 11 months) and Richard are already flagged, and any similar conversations going forward should be captured in the same way.

3

Create a structured follow-up sequence for the growth-gated pipeline — Richard (3–6 months), Jason (3–4 months), Suzette (6 months), and Joshua (first week of July confirmed) all require diarised outreach with the context of their original conversation carried forward, not a generic re-dial.

4

Refine the opening line for calls to ensure relevance and credibility are communicated within the first two sentences — the number of calls ending at the 'who are you and why are you calling' stage suggests the initial hook is not consistently landing before the prospect disengages.

5

Develop a tailored email follow-up template for prospects who request written information — Louisa and Jason both deferred to email, and a short, specific, outcome-led email with a clear next step will convert more of these deferrals than a standard overview document.

6

Suppress from active dialling any contact record flagged as: previously declined, no longer in role, on extended leave, or confirmed as a personal number — these records are consuming dial capacity and in some cases generating reputational risk.

Joshua Croghan · Senior Strategy & Revenue Operations Manager
26/05/2026
Situation
Joshua is Senior Strategy & Revenue Operations Manager at Swap, a company with 10 AEs in the UK and 24 in the US. They are currently using Gong as their conversation intelligence and forecasting tool. Gong was adopted under previous ownership of the tool (Joshua notes it was 'webbed over from the sales team') rather than chosen by the current RevOps function. They use HubSpot as their CRM. Gong is currently used primarily for forecasting, with call recording and AI functionality considered underutilised. Joshua is aware of alternatives including Attention and Momentum, suggesting he has already begun informal market research.
Pain
Joshua expressed several specific frustrations with Gong. First, cost: he flagged it as expensive relative to the value they are extracting. Second, weak AI and automation: Gong cannot reliably auto-populate data into HubSpot, meaning reps still have to fill in fields manually — a significant RevOps pain point. Third, poor cross-call search: you can search within a single call in Gong but not across all calls, limiting the ability to surface patterns or trends at scale. Fourth, poor data extraction: pulling data out of Gong is described as 'quite frustrating', and Joshua suggested Gong is deliberately limiting this capability, possibly to protect against churn. Fifth, information silos: insights from calls remain 'stuck in people's heads' or in siloed data rather than being accessible across the business. He gave a concrete example of wanting automated alerts sent to the partner team when a partner is mentioned on a call — something Gong cannot currently do well.
Impact
The manual data entry burden falls on the RevOps function, meaning Joshua is likely chasing reps for CRM updates rather than having them auto-populated. Strategic deal visibility is limited because call insights are not fully accessible or searchable across the team, which risks deals being mismanaged or dropped without leadership awareness. The partner team example illustrates a specific missed opportunity for cross-functional alignment — partner mentions on calls are not being surfaced automatically, which could be affecting partner revenue and relationship management. More broadly, Joshua acknowledged that in the first six months they effectively used Gong as 'the most expensive call recorder of all time', suggesting a prolonged period of poor ROI on a significant tool investment.
Critical Event
No hard deadline or regulatory trigger was mentioned. Joshua explicitly said this is not an immediate priority — other internal projects are taking precedence. He indicated that towards the end of 2026 they will 'probably be looking at alternatives.' The meeting has been booked for the first Friday of July 2026 (12:30pm), which Joshua himself proposed. This is a relatively early-stage conversation and the AE should treat this as a discovery and relationship-building meeting rather than a close opportunity in the near term.
Decision
Joshua is the RevOps lead and appears to be the primary evaluator and internal champion for any tooling decision of this kind. No other stakeholders were named explicitly, though the scale of the sales team (34 AEs across UK and US) suggests sign-off may involve sales leadership or finance. No budget figure was discussed. Joshua's framing of 'we probably wouldn't have chosen it ourselves' implies the previous Gong decision was made above or around the RevOps function, which may mean Joshua now has more influence in the next decision. No formal procurement process or RFP was mentioned. Timeline to decision is likely Q4 2026 at the earliest based on his comments.
Recommended AE Approach
Open the meeting by acknowledging the context Joshua gave on the call — he is not starting from zero and has a considered view of what Gong is doing well (forecasting) and where it is falling short (automation, cross-call search, data extraction, HubSpot sync). Do not treat this as a standard discovery call. Joshua is analytically sharp and will disengage quickly if the AE runs a generic demo.

Lead with the automation and data extraction angle, not call recording. Joshua's most vivid frustrations were about manual CRM input, inability to search across calls, and insights being siloed. Use his own example — the partner mention alert — as a demonstration anchor. Show Jiminny's ability to detect signals in calls and push structured data automatically into HubSpot without rep involvement. This maps directly to his RevOps remit.

Address the forecasting question explicitly and early. Joshua asked whether Jiminny has a forecasting solution. If Jiminny has this capability, lead with it or have it ready to demonstrate. If it is limited, be honest and come prepared with how Jiminny complements rather than replaces a forecasting layer — potentially positioning Jiminny's deal and CRM data hygiene as what makes forecasting more accurate, even in another tool.

On cost, the SDR mentioned 90% of Gong's functionality at 50% of the cost. The AE should be ready to substantiate this with specifics — ideally a ballpark figure or ROI framing relevant to a 34-AE team. Joshua flagged cost as a concern with Gong, so this is a lever, but it needs to feel credible rather than a sales line.

Do not rush towards a close. Joshua has been clear this is a Q4 decision at earliest. The goal of the July meeting is to earn Joshua's confidence as the internal champion and leave him with enough concrete evidence — a relevant demo, a clear feature comparison, and honest answers on forecasting — that when the formal evaluation begins, Jiminny is already the front-runner.
