Campaign Overview
May 2026 was a month defined more by market conditions than by resistance to Jiminny itself. The overall tone across conversations was polite but deflective — prospects were rarely hostile, yet the vast majority were unable to engage meaningfully, whether due to personal circumstances, organisational constraints, or timing. What stood out most was how frequently the blockers were structural rather than attitudinal: people in transition, teams locked into existing contracts, companies mid-cost-cut, and decision-makers sitting in the wrong geography entirely. The market did not push back hard on Jiminny's proposition — it simply wasn't ready to receive it.

Common Objections
Already using a competing tool ~3 conversations
"They are already sorted with Gong and expanding their contract. No interest in alternatives at this time."

This is the most commercially significant objection in the dataset. Prospects locked into Gong are not just satisfied — they are deepening their investment. Jiminny messaging needs to address this head-on, ideally with a clear articulation of what it offers that Gong does not, particularly around specific use cases such as forecasting accuracy or manager-led coaching workflows. Timing plays a role here too — approaching these accounts at or near contract renewal would be far more productive.

Team too small for the solution ~3 conversations
"Call recording analysis isn't relevant for them due to their manageable size of six sales reps."

Multiple prospects self-disqualified on the basis of team size, with one rep-count as low as two. This points to a targeting issue as much as an objection — if small teams are consistently reaching this conclusion, it may be worth reviewing the minimum viable team size within the ICP criteria and filtering these accounts out earlier in the prospecting process.

Not the right person / decision made elsewhere ~5 conversations
"I need to reach someone in the US for further discussions."

A recurring pattern throughout May was reaching individuals who either lacked budget authority or whose decisions were made centrally — often in a US head office or by a revenue operations lead. This suggests that contact-level targeting may need to be adjusted to prioritise VP-level or RevOps titles with confirmed regional autonomy, rather than relying on job title alone as a qualifier.

Currently in cost-cutting mode ~2 conversations
"They are currently in a cost-cutting phase and not interested in new investments."

Budget freeze objections are difficult to overcome in the short term but are time-bound by nature. These prospects are worth retaining in a nurture sequence with a planned re-engagement in Q4, as Laura specifically suggested 'the end of the year' as a natural re-evaluation point. The key is maintaining a light-touch presence without applying pressure.

Internal team already handles this ~2 conversations
"They have a large internal sales enablement team handling coaching and forecasting, so it's not relevant for her."

Prospects with dedicated enablement functions often perceive external tools as redundant rather than complementary. Jiminny's positioning in these conversations may benefit from reframing the solution as an augmentation of — rather than a replacement for — internal capability, with a focus on what human teams cannot do at scale or with consistency.

Bad timing / personal circumstances ~6 conversations
"He's interested but can't engage right now due to ongoing interviews."

A significant proportion of May's call outcomes were derailed not by disinterest but by personal or situational factors — illness, maternity leave, role transitions, and mid-meeting interruptions. While these cannot be predicted, they do reinforce the importance of a robust follow-up cadence. Several of these prospects explicitly asked to be contacted again, making them warm re-engagement targets rather than closed opportunities.

What Prospects Asked
Can you call back at a better time? ~6 conversations

This was the most common prospect-initiated response across the month, arising from genuine busyness rather than avoidance. Its frequency suggests that many contacts were being reached at sub-optimal moments in their working day. Testing different call windows — particularly earlier morning slots or end-of-week timings — could improve initial engagement quality.

Who should you actually be speaking to instead? ~4 conversations

Several prospects actively redirected the call, suggesting a genuine awareness that the solution might be relevant elsewhere in their organisation. Names such as Mark, John Healy in the UK, and the revenue operations lead at Doxel were offered. These referrals, even when informal, are high-value signals and should be acted upon promptly with a warm introduction framing.

Are we already a customer of yours? ~1 conversations

One call revealed that the prospect was already a Jiminny customer, which was not flagged prior to the outreach. This highlights the importance of cross-referencing the target list against the existing customer base before dialling, both to avoid the embarrassment of cold-calling existing clients and to identify potential expansion opportunities that should be handled through account management rather than new business outreach.

Market Themes
Gong's market grip remains strong

Across conversations where incumbents were named, Gong dominated — and crucially, prospects were not merely using it but actively expanding their investment. This is a market entrenchment dynamic, not simple preference. Jiminny's outreach into Gong-heavy accounts needs a distinct competitive narrative that speaks specifically to what Gong does not do, rather than a general value proposition.

Decision-making authority is frequently misaligned with contact targeting

A recurring theme in May was reaching people who either lacked the authority to act or whose decisions were controlled by a central function — typically in the US or within a revenue operations team. This structural mismatch is costing dial efficiency. Tightening ICP criteria to target regionally autonomous decision-makers, particularly those with direct ownership of sales coaching or forecasting budgets, would likely improve conversion rates meaningfully.

Small and partner-led organisations are consistently self-disqualifying

Several prospects acknowledged Jiminny's relevance in principle but concluded it simply didn't apply to their context — either because their team was too small to justify the tooling or because their go-to-market model was partner-driven rather than direct. These are not objections to be overcome; they are genuine fit issues. Removing these account types from the prospecting list upstream would free up dial capacity for higher-probability targets.

A meaningful pool of 'not right now' prospects is building

Multiple conversations this month ended with a specific future date offered by the prospect — end of year, six to eight months, next month after a new role is settled. Rather than treating these as soft rejections, they represent a warm pipeline that, if managed carefully, could convert in Q3 or Q4. A structured re-engagement sequence with personalised context would be the most effective way to capitalise on this.

Personal and situational disruptions are suppressing conversation quality

Illness, maternity leave, role changes, and mid-meeting calls accounted for a surprisingly high share of non-conversations in May. While unavoidable individually, their collective impact on this month's output was significant. Building a slightly larger contact pool with backup names per account would provide more dialling resilience when primary contacts are unavailable for extended periods.

Recommended Actions
1

Pursue all named referrals from May immediately — John Healy in the UK, Mark (flagged by Jamie), and the revenue operations lead at Doxel were all offered as warm introductions and should be contacted with a clear reference to how the connection was made.

2

Build a structured re-engagement sequence for all 'not right now' prospects who gave a specific return date — including Roger (new VP of Sales role), Laura (end of year budget review), Joe (six to eight months), and Tom (follow-up post-illness) — with personalised context included in each touchpoint.

3

Review the target account list to remove companies with sub-ten-person sales teams and those operating primarily through channel or partner models, as these are consistently self-disqualifying and reducing overall dial efficiency.

4

Develop a Gong-specific competitive narrative for use when prospects cite Gong as their incumbent — focusing on a precise use case where Jiminny delivers differentiated value, rather than a broad comparison, to give reps a credible response to the most common barrier in the market right now.

5

Cross-reference the full prospect list against Jiminny's existing customer base before dialling to prevent cold-calling active customers — one such instance occurred in May and risks undermining trust with accounts that should be managed through a different channel entirely.

6

Audit contact-level targeting criteria to prioritise individuals with confirmed regional budget autonomy and direct ownership of sales coaching or revenue forecasting, reducing the frequency of calls that are redirected to US head offices or centralised RevOps functions.
