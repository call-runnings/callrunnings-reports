Campaign Overview
May 2026 was a month defined by access and timing challenges more than outright rejection. A significant proportion of conversations ended not because prospects were opposed to Jiminny, but because the moment simply wasn't right — people were between roles, unwell, heading into meetings, travelling, or on leave. The market felt busy and somewhat distracted, and this flavour of resistance was consistent across the board. Where genuine conversations did take place, the responses were largely civil and considered, suggesting that awareness of Jiminny as a brand is present in the market, even if that didn't translate into pipeline this month.

Common Objections
Already using a competitor solution ~4 conversations
"They are already sorted with Gong and expanding their contract."

This is the most commercially significant objection of the month. Prospects are not just sitting with existing tools — they are actively renewing and expanding them, which narrows the window for displacement. Messaging that speaks directly to what Jiminny offers that Gong does not, particularly around ease of use, pricing, or specific features, would help create a reason to at least have the conversation.

Not the right time or bad timing ~8 conversations
"He mentioned he isn't available at the moment. He asked me to call back next week."

A high volume of conversations were cut short by timing rather than genuine disinterest. This is a positive signal in the sense that prospects were not hostile, but it does suggest the follow-up cadence needs to be robust. Every 'call me back' commitment should be treated as a warm lead and actioned promptly.

Cost-cutting or budget freeze ~2 conversations
"They are currently in a cost-cutting phase and not interested in new investments."

Budget constraints are surfacing in a small but notable number of accounts. This objection is worth monitoring — if it increases in frequency over coming months, it may reflect broader market conditions. For now, it is worth having a value-led response prepared that speaks to ROI and efficiency gains rather than leading with product features.

Team too small or solution not relevant ~3 conversations
"Call recording analysis isn't relevant for them due to their manageable size of six sales reps."

Several prospects self-disqualified on the basis of team size, suggesting that either targeting is pulling in companies below the ideal customer profile threshold, or that there is a perception issue around minimum viable team size. Tightening the target list to organisations with larger sales functions would reduce this friction.

Decision sits elsewhere (wrong contact or geography) ~4 conversations
"I need to reach someone in the US for further discussions."

A recurring pattern of being directed to head offices, US-based decision-makers, or revenue operations leads suggests that the contact data may not always be mapping to the right level of seniority or the right geography. Where referrals to specific individuals or departments are given, these should be actively pursued as they represent a warmer route in than a cold dial.

Internal team handles this already ~3 conversations
"They have a large internal sales enablement team handling coaching and forecasting, so it's not relevant for her."

Where companies have invested in building internal capability, they are unlikely to be receptive to an external platform unless there is a clear gap in what their team delivers. Messaging for this persona might focus on how Jiminny complements rather than replaces internal functions, or on the scalability and consistency benefits that even well-resourced teams struggle to maintain manually.

What Prospects Asked
Can you call back at a better time? ~6 conversations

The frequency of this soft deflection indicates that prospects are not immediately dismissive but are genuinely pressed for time. This is an invitation rather than a rejection, and following up with discipline on each of these instances could convert a proportion of them into conversations with a warmer starting point.

Who should you actually be speaking to? ~4 conversations

Multiple prospects redirected calls to other individuals or departments, suggesting that contact mapping needs refinement. However, each redirect is also a small piece of organisational intelligence — understanding who owns the sales technology decision in each account is valuable even when the current contact is not the right person.

Market Themes
Gong entrenchment is the primary competitive barrier

Across several accounts, Gong was not simply in place but actively being expanded and deepened. This goes beyond standard incumbent resistance — these companies are doubling down on their existing investment, which makes a near-term switch unlikely. The practical implication is that Gong accounts may need a longer nurture cycle, with outreach timed around renewal windows rather than cold introductions.

Organisational complexity is routing decisions away from frontline contacts

A consistent pattern this month involved being redirected to US head offices, revenue operations functions, or senior leaders in other geographies. This suggests that sales technology decisions are increasingly centralised and that reaching the right stakeholder requires more precise targeting at the outset. Where referrals to specific individuals were given, these should be followed up as high-priority leads.

Team size and relevance perception is filtering out smaller accounts

Several prospects disqualified themselves on the basis that their team was too small to benefit from Jiminny's proposition. Whether or not this is accurate, the perception is real and is causing early exits from conversations. Reviewing the minimum team size threshold in the target criteria, and considering whether messaging needs to address smaller teams differently, would help reduce this form of self-disqualification.

A notable portion of non-conversions were circumstantial rather than commercial

Transitions between roles, illness, parental leave, and mid-travel encounters accounted for a meaningful share of conversations that went nowhere this month. These are not lost accounts — they are deferred conversations, and a structured follow-up approach timed appropriately could reactivate a good number of them over the next one to two months.

Data quality is reducing the effective reach of the campaign

Several dials landed on entirely wrong contacts — a self-employed lawyer, a person who had already left the business, and existing customers who were inadvertently included. Each misdirected call represents not just a wasted dial but a potential reputational risk if the same contacts are reached repeatedly. A data audit ahead of June would likely improve both efficiency and the quality of first impressions.

Recommended Actions
1

Conduct a data quality audit on the contact list before June — remove confirmed leavers, existing customers, and contacts below the ideal company size threshold to reduce wasted dials and improve the signal-to-noise ratio in conversations.

2

Build a structured follow-up sequence for all 'call me back' and 'not now' outcomes from May — including Roger (settling into new VP of Sales role), Tom (recovering from illness), Laura (end-of-year budget review), Joe (six to eight months), and Melvin (requested callback next week) — as these represent the warmest near-term opportunities.

3

Develop a specific response to the Gong objection that does not attempt to displace it head-on, but instead identifies a gap or complementary use case — prospects who are expanding Gong contracts are unlikely to switch, but may be open to a conversation framed around what Gong does not cover.

4

Pursue the referrals received during May as priority outbound targets: John Healy in the UK (referred by Alex), the revenue operations lead at Doxel (referred by a RevOps-adjacent contact), and Mark (referred by Jamie who agreed to pass along information directly).

5

Review the targeting criteria to ensure contact records map to individuals with direct ownership of sales technology or coaching decisions — the volume of redirections to US offices and revenue operations functions suggests that current contacts are often one or two steps removed from the actual decision-maker.
