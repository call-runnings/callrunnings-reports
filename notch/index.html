Campaign Overview
June 2026 was a month defined by a market that is deeply engaged with AI — but largely on its own terms. The vast majority of conversations revealed prospects who are not ignoring the space; quite the opposite. They are actively implementing, experimenting, and in many cases already running internal AI programmes. The challenge this month was not scepticism about AI agents, but rather the confidence — sometimes self-assurance — of teams who believe they have the problem solved in-house. The pipeline that did open came from prospects willing to pause and consider whether their current approach might benefit from external expertise, even if they needed a little time to get there.

Common Objections
Already built it in-house ~8 conversations
"We have a dedicated internal AI Ops team but agreed to review more information via email."

This was the dominant objection of the month. Prospects across varying company sizes and sectors have invested in internal capability and feel ownership over their AI stack. The objection is rarely hostile — it is confident. The opportunity lies in reframing the conversation away from replacement and towards risk reduction or acceleration. Prospects who have built in-house are often unaware of the failure modes they have not yet encountered. Messaging that leads with 'common mistakes' or 'what we see go wrong at your stage' is more likely to create a crack in this objection than any capability claim.

Not the right person / wrong contact ~4 conversations
"He's not involved with the relevant teams anymore. He suggested I reach out to founder Carlos in Madrid."

Several calls landed on contacts who had moved on, changed roles, or were never the correct decision-maker for AI infrastructure. This is a list quality and targeting signal as much as an objection. Each redirect — to a CTO, an IT Ops lead, a founder — is valuable intelligence that should be captured and actioned immediately. The referrals provided this month represent warm paths into accounts that would otherwise remain cold.

Wrong time — other priorities or budget constraints ~5 conversations
"They currently have other priority projects and lack the budget and bandwidth for new initiatives."

A cluster of prospects acknowledged the relevance of AI agents but cited competing internal demands. This is a timing objection, not a fit objection. These contacts have not ruled Notch out — they have deferred. The appropriate response is a structured follow-up sequence that keeps Notch visible without being intrusive, timed to the windows prospects themselves named (end of July, September, October).

Satisfied with current AI trajectory ~3 conversations
"They have already invested heavily in Claude and are satisfied with their current trajectory."

A more entrenched version of the in-house objection, where the prospect is not just building but actively progressing and feels momentum. These contacts are harder to move in the short term but are worth monitoring — satisfaction with a self-built trajectory tends to erode as complexity scales. A light-touch nurture approach positioning Notch as a future safety net is more appropriate than pushing for an immediate meeting.

Personal number — remove me ~2 conversations
"She requested to be removed from the database as it was her personal number."

Two separate contacts were reached on personal numbers and immediately requested removal. This is a data hygiene issue with direct implications for brand perception and compliance. Any contact reached on a personal number who has not opted in should be flagged and removed immediately, and the source of those numbers reviewed to prevent recurrence.

Prefers email over cold calls ~4 conversations
"He doesn't answer cold calls. He asked me to send an email instead, stating he would review it and respond if there's interest."

A meaningful minority of prospects are not opposed to Notch's proposition but are opposed to the channel. This is not a disqualification — it is a channel preference that should be respected and used. Every prospect who requests an email has provided a pathway; the quality and relevance of what is sent matters enormously. Personalised, concise follow-ups tied specifically to what was said on the call will outperform generic outreach.

What Prospects Asked
Can you send me more information by email first? ~7 conversations

The most common response to a cold introduction was a request to receive information before committing to a conversation. This reveals a market that is cautious with its time but not closed to the proposition. Prospects want to evaluate on their own terms before agreeing to a call. It also suggests the verbal pitch alone is not yet landing the hook — there is a need for a sharp, well-structured leave-behind document or one-pager that can do the work asynchronously.

Can you call me back at a better time? ~5 conversations

Multiple prospects asked to be called back — some with specific windows (after June 8th, in a few months, September or October). This is a positive signal in aggregate: these prospects did not hang up or refuse to engage. They are managing their calendar, not dismissing the topic. Each of these represents a concrete follow-up task with a named date, and the quality of the callback will determine whether this interest converts.

What specifically do you offer around Claude / AI agent deployment? ~2 conversations

Where prospects did engage substantively, questions focused on what Notch's offering looked like in the context of tools they already use — particularly Claude. This signals that generic AI messaging is less effective than Claude-specific positioning. Prospects who are already in the ecosystem want to know what Notch adds to something they are already using, not a replacement or a parallel track.

Are you the same company as Notch (prospect confusion)? ~1 conversations

One call produced confusion between the calling party identifying as Notch and the prospect's own context. This may reflect a targeting issue where a prospect's company name or brand overlaps with the Notch identity, or it may indicate that the opening line did not clearly establish who was calling and why. Worth monitoring for recurrence.

Market Themes
The 'We've Already Done It' Market

The standout theme of June was the prevalence of prospects who have not just heard of AI agents but have already deployed them — internally, with custom builds, or through platforms like Claude and Zoom. This is a market that has moved faster than many anticipated, and the early self-builders are now in a phase of consolidation rather than exploration. The implication for Notch is that the pitch cannot start from 'here is what AI agents can do' — it must start from 'here is what typically goes wrong once you've built one.'

Claude as the Default Incumbent

Claude appeared more than any other named tool across the transcripts, and consistently as a source of confidence rather than frustration. Prospects who use Claude feel they have made a serious, credible choice and are progressing well. This creates both a barrier and an opportunity: Notch can reposition around being Claude-specialist expertise — not a competitor to Claude, but a layer of experience that helps teams get more from it safely and at scale.

Q3 Is When the Conversations Restart

A notable number of prospects named specific future windows — late July, September, October — as when they would be ready to revisit AI investment conversations. This clustering suggests that June sits at the tail end of a planning cycle, with teams heads-down on current projects and not open to new initiatives until Q3. The callbacks and LinkedIn connections made this month are seeds for a more receptive market in autumn, and the follow-up discipline between now and then will determine how many of those seeds convert.

Organisational Churn Is Disrupting Decision-Making

Several calls uncovered contacts who were leaving their companies, mid-restructure, or no longer involved in the relevant teams. This level of personnel movement means that even well-researched contact lists can go stale quickly. It also means that referrals gathered during these calls — to CTOs, IT Ops leads, and founders — carry genuine value as navigational intelligence within accounts.

The Technical Founder Cohort Is Self-Sufficient — For Now

A subset of prospects came from founding teams or early-stage companies with in-house engineering capability, recent funding, and a strong conviction that they can build and maintain their own AI infrastructure. These are not the right targets for an immediate conversion, but they are worth tracking. As their AI programmes scale and complexity compounds, the case for external expertise typically strengthens. Notch would benefit from a nurture track specifically designed for this cohort.

Situation

Pain
Two distinct pain contexts emerged. First, for IVigee Digital specifically: no pain was surfaced — they already have a large dev team and are fully resourced on AI. John explicitly closed that door. Second, and more relevantly for Notch, the pain John articulated was around the wider market he operates in — sales teams not reaching out, over-relying on technology, and unqualified pipeline being generated by junior BDRs who lack commercial judgement. He said: 'Half the problems I had with teams that I put in place, they're just not reaching out. Calling people, emailing, they're just expecting technology to do it for them.' He also described the broader RevOps/GTM AI deployment pain Sean opened with — integrations, guardrails, CRM hygiene, and adoption — as familiar and credible, even if not personally acute at IVigee right now.
Impact
The operational impact John references is around wasted pipeline capacity and poor qualification in the teams he builds and advises. He told one of his sales reps this morning to 'stop filling your diary with useless crap' — indicating that low-quality, unqualified activity is a live issue in the companies he works with. Strategically, John is well-positioned as a connector and influencer: he is regularly approached for introductions and referrals at CRO/RevOps level, and explicitly said 'I get asked all the time' for exactly the kind of introductions Notch needs. The impact of winning John as a partner rather than a direct customer could be significant — he has ongoing access to multiple early-stage funded startups who are actively building commercial infrastructure and would be natural Notch prospects.

No formal deadline or trigger event was mentioned for IVigee Digital — and John ruled them out as a near-term prospect entirely due to existing funding and dev resource. For his Persuasion Jiu-Jitsu work, he mentioned 'once the politics changes, I know there'll be different opportunities' which is vague and not a firm trigger. No budget cycle, contract renewal, or regulatory deadline was referenced. The meeting was booked for Wednesday at 11:15 (implied to be 4th June 2026) — the urgency here is relationship-driven rather than event-driven. The AE should not go in expecting a deal timeline; this is a partnership/referral conversation first.
