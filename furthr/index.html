Campaign Overview

In April 2026, the Furthr campaign generated 1,606 total dials, resulting in 186 connected calls and 145 full conversations. From those conversations, 9 meetings were booked and 26 prospects were marked as Activated — indicating meaningful engagement or expressed future interest. A further 25 prospects responded with 'No, Not Now', suggesting timing rather than fundamental disinterest. 63 prospects were categorised as 'No, Not Ever', reflecting a firm rejection of the proposition. Of the 21 prospects who indicated the call had reached the wrong person, 8 provided a referral to a more relevant contact, while 13 did not. Overall, the campaign delivered a solid base of market intelligence alongside its pipeline activity, with the conversation-to-meeting rate reflecting a competitive and well-defended market.

Competitor & Market Intelligence

Several named and unnamed third-party solutions were referenced by prospects during conversations, offering a useful view of the competitive landscape Furthr operates within.

Mondra was mentioned explicitly by one prospect, who noted they are under a long-term contract with the platform, making any near-term switch effectively off the table. The sentiment was not negative toward Furthr, but the contractual lock-in presented a structural barrier.

PlanetMark was referenced by a prospect currently using them for carbon accounting. Interestingly, this prospect was not entirely closed — they indicated they are not looking to expand until autumn and expressed a specific interest in breaking down emissions by fuel type, a capability gap worth noting. A follow-up meeting was scheduled from this conversation.

SAP was mentioned in the context of a larger organisation's scope three reporting infrastructure, where a third-party solution had been integrated directly into SAP systems. This signals that in enterprise environments, carbon reporting tools are often evaluated through the lens of ERP compatibility.

Schneider Electric was cited not as a direct competitor but as a prior employer of a sustainability manager, who felt their experience there had equipped them sufficiently for their current role — suggesting that platform familiarity and professional confidence can act as soft barriers to adoption.

Several prospects referenced unnamed consultants and internal tools as their current solution, with phrases such as 'we already have consultants and internal tools' and 'we write our own code' indicating a preference in some organisations for bespoke or advisory-led approaches over SaaS platforms. The perception that platforms in this space can be 'too expensive and lacking needed flexibility' was raised directly by one prospect, which is a meaningful signal about how Furthr may be positioned relative to custom-built alternatives.

Common Objections

The following objections recurred consistently across the April conversations and reveal important structural dynamics in the market.

1. Already Have a Solution in Place
This was the single most common objection, appearing across multiple call types and sectors. Prospects cited consultants, internal tools, bespoke code, and named platforms as reasons they were not actively looking. In several cases, these arrangements were contractually fixed for one to three years, making near-term conversion unlikely regardless of proposition strength. This suggests a market with relatively high incumbent stickiness.

2. Not the Right Person / Handled Elsewhere
A notable portion of calls reached individuals who confirmed sustainability and carbon reporting sit with a different team — often head office, a dedicated ESG function, or a named colleague. This reflects the growing specialisation of sustainability roles within larger organisations and suggests that contact data may benefit from more granular role-level targeting.

3. Too Busy / Bad Timing
Many prospects did not object to the proposition itself but simply could not engage at the time of the call. Callbacks were requested ranging from later the same day to the following week, indicating genuine openness in some cases but a fragmented ability to commit time to new conversations.

4. Not Relevant to Our Business Model
Several prospects indicated that the nature of their organisation — including a social housing provider and a small charity — meant their carbon reporting requirements were limited in scope or volume, and that Furthr's solution felt designed for a different type of organisation. This points to a potential need for sector-specific messaging.

5. Cold Call Aversion / TPS Registration
A small number of prospects declined to engage on principle, citing TPS registration or a general preference against cold calling. One prospect hung up immediately upon being told it was a cold call. These contacts represent hard stops rather than addressable objections.
""Their focus on social housing doesn't require much on carbon capture — they work with a consultant and don't have extensive reporting needs like the private sector.""
""Sustainability reporting and carbon data management are handled by their head office, and he doesn't get involved in those matters.""
""They don't take cold calls as they are registered with the telephone preference service.""
""They are not in the market for our services and did not want to engage further.""

Prospect Questions

While full prospect questions were not always captured verbatim in the transcripts, recurring themes in prospect responses reveal the questions and concerns that were active in their minds during these conversations.

Data Source and Contact Origin
One prospect, Jasmine, directly asked how her personal number had been obtained — a question that reflects growing awareness around data privacy and the provenance of contact databases. This was handled constructively in the call, with the rep offering guidance on how she could remove her details from data sources.

Scope Three Complexity and Future Readiness
Several prospects who were not immediately interested still referenced their scope three emissions workload as an area of anticipated growth or challenge. One contact at a charity acknowledged that 'data management will become more challenging as data volume increases' — signalling awareness of a future pain point even where no immediate need existed.

Breaking Down Emissions by Fuel Type
One prospect with a current PlanetMark arrangement specifically flagged an interest in granular emissions breakdowns by fuel type as a capability they would want in future. This is a specific, functional question that reveals where existing solutions may be falling short.

Platform Flexibility and Cost
The prospect who builds their own internal tools raised a pointed concern about whether third-party platforms offer sufficient flexibility relative to their cost. This implies a question of 'why would we pay for something we can customise ourselves?' that Furthr's proposition may need to address directly in future conversations.

Market Themes

Across the full set of April conversations, several broader themes emerged that reflect where the sustainability reporting market currently sits.

Maturity is Uneven Across Organisations
The market is clearly not at a uniform stage of readiness. Some prospects have operated dedicated carbon emissions programmes for several years across all scopes, while others — particularly smaller charities and organisations in sectors like social housing — are managing reporting manually with Excel and an associate. This unevenness suggests that a single-message approach is unlikely to resonate across the full addressable market.

Scope Three is a Growing But Still Complex Area
Scope three emissions appeared repeatedly as both a current workload and a future concern. Some organisations have delegated it to colleagues or third parties; others are actively working on it but feel they have it under control. The language around scope three was notably more cautious and complex than that around scope one and two, suggesting it remains the area of greatest uncertainty — and therefore greatest opportunity — for a data solution.

Contractual Lock-In is a Significant Timing Constraint
Multiple prospects referenced existing contracts ranging from long-term Mondra arrangements to unnamed three-year agreements. This is less a reflection of satisfaction with current solutions and more a structural timing issue. The market may be more accessible in 12 to 18 months as these contracts come up for renewal.

Internally Built Solutions Signal a Sophistication Gap
Several organisations have invested in writing their own code or developing internal systems for carbon tracking. This reflects a segment of the market that is technically sophisticated but potentially under-served by off-the-shelf platforms. Their objections are not about the need for better data management — they are about whether any external platform can match the specificity of what they have built themselves.

Role Fragmentation is Increasing
A recurring pattern across calls was the diffusion of sustainability responsibilities — with scope one and two handled by one team, scope three by another, and overall ESG strategy sitting at head office or with a separate function. This fragmentation means that reaching the right decision-maker requires more precise targeting and that any single contact is unlikely to represent the full buying picture.
""Their team has been working on carbon emissions for several years and doesn't need assistance — they focus on scope one and two, while colleagues handle the broader scope three with third-party integration into SAP systems.""
""Managing scope three emissions has been manageable due to cooperative contractors — currently using an associate for calculations and Excel, but expects data management to become more challenging as data volume increases.""
""Their previous experience at Schneider Electric has left them well-equipped for their current role and they are strong in scope three reporting.""

Recommended Actions

Based on the intelligence gathered across April's conversations, we recommend the following actions for the Furthr campaign going forward.

1. Build a Contract Renewal Tracking Sequence
Given the frequency with which prospects cited existing contracts as a barrier — including named multi-year agreements — we recommend establishing a structured follow-up sequence for these contacts timed to their anticipated contract renewal windows. Where a timeframe was mentioned (e.g. 'not until autumn', 'three-year contract'), those prospects should be flagged and re-engaged at the appropriate point with a targeted message about re-evaluation.

2. Develop Sector-Specific Messaging for Lower-Complexity Organisations
Several prospects in sectors such as social housing, charities, and smaller organisations indicated that Furthr's proposition felt designed for larger or more complex businesses. A lighter-touch or entry-level value narrative that speaks to organisations managing reporting manually — and anticipating growth in data volume — could unlock a segment that is currently rejecting the conversation too early.

3. Address the Flexibility and Cost Perception Directly
The objection that platforms in this space are 'too expensive and lacking needed flexibility' relative to bespoke internal solutions is specific enough to warrant a direct response in the pitch. We recommend developing a clear, concise rebuttal or proof point — ideally supported by a case study — that demonstrates Furthr's adaptability and total cost of ownership relative to in-house development.

4. Sharpen Contact Targeting to Reach Scope Three Decision-Makers
A significant number of conversations were redirected because the contact reached did not own the sustainability reporting function — or specifically did not own scope three. Given that scope three is Furthr's most relevant territory, we recommend refining the contact criteria to prioritise titles and functions directly associated with supply chain emissions, ESG data, or sustainability reporting rather than broader sustainability management.

5. Capitalise on the Fuel-Type Breakdown Interest as a Conversation Hook
One prospect explicitly raised the desire to break down emissions by fuel type as an unmet need within their current platform. This is a specific, functional capability gap that may resonate with other prospects in similar positions. We recommend testing whether this use case — granular fuel-type emissions analysis — can be introduced earlier in the pitch as a hook for prospects who believe their current solution is 'good enough', as it speaks to a level of analytical depth that generic carbon accounting tools may not offer.
