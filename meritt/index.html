Campaign Overview
April 2026 was a month defined by structural friction rather than outright rejection. The market felt cautious — not hostile — with the majority of conversations landing in a holding pattern of internal restructuring, recent hires, or acquisition uncertainty. Where doors opened, they opened thoughtfully: prospects were willing to talk, willing to connect, and in several cases willing to revisit in a defined timeframe. That considered receptiveness is a meaningful signal beneath what the headline numbers might suggest.

Common Objections
Not hiring right now ~12 conversations
"They have closed vacancies currently and aren't hiring for sales roles. Future hiring depends on the performance of their existing sales team."

This was the dominant objection of the month and in most cases reflected a genuine temporary pause rather than a fundamental lack of need. Prospects like Martina, Jess, and Sue all indicated conditions under which hiring would resume. This objection should be treated as a timing challenge, not a closed door — the priority is to secure specific follow-up dates rather than accepting a vague 'not now'.

We handle recruitment internally ~5 conversations
"They currently use an internal recruitment company and primarily post ads on LinkedIn."

Several organisations have invested in internal talent functions and use that as a reason to disengage. Notably, at least one internal stakeholder (Pamela at Mytek) personally disagreed with this policy — suggesting there is latent dissatisfaction with the internal model that isn't being surfaced. Messaging that speaks to what internal teams can't do — speed, specialist networks, AI screening — may help reframe this objection.

Wrong person / not involved in hiring ~4 conversations
"He indicated he isn't the right person to discuss hiring plans and referenced an internal talent team, though he couldn't recall their names."

A recurring issue was reaching contacts who had no visibility into hiring decisions or who had moved roles entirely. This points to a contact data quality issue rather than a market receptiveness issue. Improving targeting to verified hiring managers and HR leads would reduce wasted conversations significantly.

Recent hire or team already built out ~4 conversations
"They've recently hired and have a talent acquisition team in place, so there's no current opportunity for further conversation."

Some prospects had genuinely just completed a hiring cycle and were in a consolidation phase. This is a natural market rhythm. These contacts are worth flagging for re-engagement in Q3/Q4 once new headcount plans typically form, rather than being marked as permanently closed.

Acquisition, restructure, or administration ~3 conversations
"They are not hiring currently due to a potential acquisition and may be looking for a new role himself."

Organisational instability — whether acquisition, restructure, or in one case administration — rendered several conversations unproductive through no fault of the proposition. These contacts should be monitored rather than followed up immediately; once structural uncertainty resolves, hiring activity often accelerates sharply.

Only hires people they already know ~2 conversations
"He's currently developing a partner network and will only hire trusted individuals he already knows for now."

This trust-based hiring objection typically reflects early-stage companies where founders are still building their core team personally. It is a genuine cultural barrier in the short term. Positioning merit.io as a discovery tool for warm introductions — rather than cold candidate supply — may be more compelling for this audience.

What Prospects Asked
Can you send company details and candidate profiles? ~2 conversations

Peter's request for written materials and candidate profiles before involving his hiring team is a classic evaluation step — it suggests genuine interest but a need to socialise the proposition internally. This is a strong buying signal and indicates that a well-structured one-pager or sample candidate deck could be a meaningful conversion tool.

What is merit.io and how does the job board work? ~3 conversations

References to merit.io came up organically across several calls — both as a tool offered to displaced salespeople and as a resource mentioned to hiring managers. The fact that it is being introduced mid-conversation suggests prospects are receptive once they hear about it, but it may not be leading the pitch strongly enough. Proactively positioning the scale of the platform (30,000 candidates was mentioned in one call) earlier in conversations could accelerate interest.

How do you screen candidates — what's the AI element? ~1 conversations

Barry raised the importance of maintaining a human element alongside AI in recruitment, implying he had questions about how the screening process works in practice. This suggests that where AI-assisted screening is part of the Meritt proposition, prospects want reassurance that it enhances rather than replaces human judgement. A clear, confident explanation of the process could neutralise this concern before it becomes a blocker.

Market Themes
The market is paused, not closed

The overwhelming tone of April's conversations was one of temporary suspension rather than structural disinterest. Hiring freezes, team restructures, and fiscal year transitions are creating a gap between acknowledged need and active buying. Prospects are not saying sales recruitment is irrelevant — they are saying the timing is wrong. This distinction matters enormously for how the pipeline is managed over the coming quarter.

Internal recruitment is the dominant incumbent

Across multiple conversations, in-house talent teams and LinkedIn job posting emerged as the default incumbent rather than any named external recruiter. This means Meritt is frequently competing not against another agency but against the perceived sufficiency of doing it internally. The commercial case for Meritt needs to address this directly — particularly around speed, quality, and the hidden cost of internal-only hiring.

Contact data quality is limiting reach

A notable portion of April's calls were lost to stale data — people who had left companies, organisations in administration, incorrect contacts, and unreachable numbers. This is suppressing the campaign's true conversion rate on live, relevant prospects. Improving data hygiene and implementing a pre-call verification step where possible would materially improve the efficiency of each dialling session.

Displaced salespeople are an untapped audience touchpoint

Several calls unexpectedly landed on salespeople who had recently been made redundant or were between roles. In each case, merit.io was offered as a resource and received warmly. While these individuals are not immediate revenue opportunities, they represent a goodwill channel and a potential talent supply pipeline that reinforces the platform's value to hiring managers. A lightweight process for capturing these contacts could build long-term brand equity.

Future pipeline is building through agreed follow-ups

A healthy number of prospects — including Martina, Barry (at iBright Utilities), Sue, and Jason — agreed to specific future touchpoints, with timelines ranging from four weeks to six months. This represents a structured pipeline of warm re-engagements rather than cold outreach, and should be treated as a priority follow-up list heading into May and beyond.
