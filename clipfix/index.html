Campaign Overview
April 2026 was a month defined by a crowded market and well-defended status quos. The majority of conversations revealed prospects who are not in active buying mode — not because the problem Clipfix solves is unfamiliar to them, but because many believe they already have it covered. Video, diagnostics, and remote troubleshooting are concepts that have landed in this market; the challenge is that prospects are pointing to a wide variety of incumbent tools — from WhatsApp and generic video conferencing platforms to bespoke ERP-integrated diagnostic software — as evidence that the need is met. The overall atmosphere was polite but resistant, with very few conversations developing into genuine discovery.

Common Objections
We already have a solution in place ~10 conversations
"Their products already have advanced diagnostic software, making my solution less relevant."

This was the single most common reason for rejection this month, surfacing across sectors and roles. The objection is rarely about price or timing — it is about perceived redundancy. Prospects are not saying the problem does not exist; they are saying it is already solved. This means the sales conversation needs to shift from problem-awareness to differentiation: what does Clipfix do that their current tool — whether WhatsApp, Grip, or an ERP integration — cannot? Specific capability gaps, measurable outcomes, and case comparisons will be essential to breaking through this objection.

Not relevant to my role ~5 conversations
"He clarified that he works in industrial commercial, not housing, and stated their operations are already effective with good software in place."

A recurring theme was reaching contacts who were either in the wrong department or whose job function simply did not intersect with the Clipfix use case. Maintenance, warehousing, and industrial commercial roles came up specifically. This points to a contact data and targeting issue as much as a messaging one. Tightening the ideal contact profile — likely customer service leadership, technical support management, or field service operations — would reduce this friction significantly.

We do not take or handle many inbound calls ~2 conversations
"They provide technical support as a final line, meaning they handle few inbound calls. He felt my offering wouldn't be relevant to their operations."

Some prospects — particularly those operating as OEM last-line support or with highly engineer-led models — do not see themselves as a contact centre in the traditional sense. This suggests the Clipfix proposition may need a secondary framing for field-service-heavy businesses, emphasising remote triage and engineer dispatch reduction rather than call centre efficiency.

Group or committee decision-making means I cannot act unilaterally ~2 conversations
"Decisions are made at a group level, making it unlikely they'd adopt changes."

In at least two conversations, the obstacle was not personal reluctance but organisational structure. One prospect explicitly framed their video platform as 'group-driven.' These are not dead-ends but longer-cycle opportunities that require multi-threaded outreach — ideally identifying who sits on the decision-making group and engaging them directly.

Recently signed a contract with an existing provider ~1 conversations
"She mentioned they already use a similar service, Grip, and just signed a new two-year contract."

Helen's situation at her company is a clear timing objection backed by a contractual lock-in. She proactively suggested a follow-up in 18 months, which is a genuine pipeline opportunity. These contacts should be placed into a long-cycle nurture sequence with a hard diary note to re-engage at the appropriate time.

What Prospects Asked
How did you get my personal contact number? ~1 conversations

Lindsay's question about data sourcing is a signal worth taking seriously. Even if asked only once explicitly, it reflects an underlying concern about data provenance that likely exists more broadly. In regulated sectors and among more senior contacts, this question can derail a call before the pitch lands. Having a clear, confident, and concise answer to this — referencing the legitimate data source — is important for maintaining credibility in the opening seconds of a call.

Can you send information by email for me to review? ~3 conversations

Several prospects — including Samson, Mark Evans, and Reese — asked for email materials rather than continuing the conversation live. This is a mixed signal: it can indicate genuine curiosity or polite deflection. In Samson's case it came with an explicit interest in MPS scores, suggesting real relevance. The follow-up emails sent in response to these requests represent warm touchpoints and should be treated as active pipeline, not just courtesy sends.

Market Themes
Video is normalised — but not standardised

Prospects across April consistently referenced video as something they already use, but the tools vary wildly: WhatsApp, Grip, proprietary OEM software, and generic conferencing platforms all came up. There is no dominant standard in place. This fragmentation is an opportunity — Clipfix can position itself as the purposebuilt alternative to ad hoc workarounds, rather than competing with a single entrenched platform.

The gatekeeper and data quality problem is compressing true conversation rates

A notable portion of dials this month resulted in gatekeepers, voicemails, retired contacts, or individuals who had left their organisations. In several cases, the person called had not worked at the target company for months or years. Improving data recency and directline access would meaningfully increase the number of decision-maker conversations available from the same volume of dials.

Referrals are an underutilised asset

Two conversations this month produced named referrals — Robert Ball at Valento (head of customer technical services) and a potential internal forward at British Gas via Reese. These warm introductions are the highest-quality leads generated in April and deserve prioritised follow-up. The fact that they emerged from conversations that were otherwise dead ends suggests a more deliberate referral-asking strategy could yield more of the same.

Sector fit matters more than vertical breadth

Conversations with contacts in warehouse operations, industrial commercial, and OEM last-line support consistently produced the objection that Clipfix is simply not relevant to their role. Conversely, conversations touching on boiler servicing, field engineering dispatch, and customer-facing technical support showed clearer resonance. Focusing outreach on sectors where inbound technical troubleshooting is a daily operational reality — rather than casting broadly — would increase conversion rates.

Prospects in contract lock-in are tomorrow's pipeline

Helen's two-year Grip contract and similar situations where prospects are recently renewed represent a distinct category of opportunity. These are not rejections — they are time-shifted possibilities. Building a structured re-engagement sequence for these contacts, timed to the end of their contract periods, would convert what currently looks like lost calls into future meetings.
